# Context-first interpretation
**Observation → multiple possible explanations → context check → safe response.** No single signal reliably reveals an internal emotion. Compare combinations of posture, eyes, tail, sounds, environment and the dog's normal baseline. [S01](../sources/S01.md) [S02](../sources/S02.md) [S04](../sources/S04.md) [S05](../sources/S05.md).

**Example:** A wagging tail with relaxed posture should not be equated with a wagging tail and rigid body. [S01](../sources/S01.md) [S02](../sources/S02.md).

Related: [Tail](tail-language.md) · [Ears and eyes](ears-and-eyes.md) · [Stress](stress-signals.md) · [Barking](barking-behavior.md).

## Mixed emotions and consent (S06)
A dog may simultaneously want to approach something rewarding and avoid an uncomfortable person or situation. Such conflict is a reason to ask what is happening around the dog rather than assign a single feeling. [S06](../sources/S06.md) (p. 6).

If a dog is uneasy, allow choice, distance and time to recover; avoid forced encounters. [S06](../sources/S06.md) (pp. 5, 8–9).
