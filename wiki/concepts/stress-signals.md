# Stress and arousal
Potential signals include lip-licking, yawning, avoidance, pinned ears, tucked tail, freezing, pacing, and vocalization. Not every instance indicates stress: compare the dog's normal behavior and immediate environment. [S01](../sources/S01.md) [S02](../sources/S02.md) [S05](../sources/S05.md).

When signs cluster, reduce pressure and give space; seek qualified help for persistent or concerning patterns. [S02](../sources/S02.md) [S05](../sources/S05.md).

Related: [Ears and eyes](ears-and-eyes.md) · [Barking](barking-behavior.md) · [Context](context-and-interpretation.md).

## New synthesis from S06 (Dogs Trust)
Stress-related behavior may arise from fear, anxiety or frustration, and a dog can experience competing motivations. Instead of interpreting one sign in isolation, examine the whole-body pattern and situation. [S06](../sources/S06.md) (pp. 6–7).

When the dog seems overwhelmed, prioritize distance from the trigger, the option to withdraw and recovery in a safe setting; do not force an interaction. [S06](../sources/S06.md) (pp. 5, 8–9).

For new or concerning behavior changes, consider veterinary assessment rather than assuming the cause is purely emotional. [S06](../sources/S06.md) (p. 13).
