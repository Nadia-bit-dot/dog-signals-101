# S01 — Dog Body Language Explained

**Source:** Stephanie Gibeault, American Kennel Club, *Dog Body Language Explained: What Is Your Dog Trying to Tell You?*  
**Original:** https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/health/how-to-read-dog-body-language/  
**Snapshot:** [PDF](../../raw/S01_AKC_Dog_Body_Language.pdf) · accessed 2026-11-23  
**Scope reviewed:** PDF pages 1–3; page 4 contains partial/overlaid webpage material and is not used for claims.

## Supported notes (paraphrased)

1. **Tail:** Wagging reflects emotional arousal, not necessarily happiness. Tail height and the dog's typical neutral tail carriage are relevant; neutral position differs by breed (PDF p.1).
2. **Raised hackles:** Piloerection indicates arousal and can accompany different emotional states, not necessarily aggression (p.2).
3. **Posture:** Lowering the body or shifting weight can indicate different responses depending on the situation; a play bow is one possible invitation to play (p.2).
4. **Face and mouth:** Yawning and lip licking can occur in stressful contexts, but must not be interpreted without context (p.2).
5. **Eyes:** Soft eyes and hard staring differ; visible eye whites (often called “whale eye”) can be a discomfort clue in some situations (p.3).
6. **Context:** No single cue tells the whole story. Evaluate posture, tail, facial expression, environment and the individual dog's baseline together (p.3).

## Boundaries and open questions

- This source does not establish a universal one-to-one translation between a behavior and an emotion.
- Barking causes and detailed ear positions are **not sufficiently covered here**; retain as gaps for later sources.
- Claims above are educational paraphrases, not diagnoses or universal rules.
